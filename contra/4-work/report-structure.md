# The Perception Gap Report — Document Structure

> **The deliverable of every Perception Audit engagement.**
> A single document, 4-6 pages, written. Complete. No open questions.

---

## What the Report Is

A precise, written diagnosis of the gap between how a business operates and how it appears publicly. Not a strategy. Not a visual recommendation. Not a roadmap. A complete account of what the gap is, where it comes from, and what it costs — documented with enough precision that the client knows exactly what they are dealing with.

The client receives a document. Not a direction. The direction is theirs to decide.

---

## The Five Sections

### Section 1 — What Your Presence Currently Signals

**Purpose:** What the market reads when it sees the business — not what the business intends to communicate.

**Content:**
- **First impression audit** — A narrative, not a checklist. What someone who looks up the business for the first time finds, and what impression forms. Described from the perspective of an external observer, not the client.
- **Involuntary signals** — What the presence communicates unintentionally. Absences (what is missing and what the absence suggests). Inconsistencies (where the presence contradicts reality). Associations (what category, level, or type of business the presence aligns with involuntarily).

**The question it answers:** *If I didn't know you at all and searched for you now — what would be there, and why would I care or not?*

**Format:** 3-5 concrete observations, each with its specific consequence. Complete sentences. Not lists.

---

### Section 2 — What Your Business Actually Is

**Purpose:** Distilled reality — not the client's self-presentation, but what the strategist understood from everything heard and seen.

**Content:**
- **What has actually been built** — Not a description of services. A description of substance. What exists in reality that does not appear publicly.
- **Why the business is difficult to replace** — Specific. Not "vast experience" or "personalized approach." Exactly what makes the business hard to copy and hard to replace.
- **Who the business actually is for the right clients** — What role it plays in the lives of the clients who work best. Not the service category — the real function.

**The question it answers:** *If I strip away every external signal, what is actually here?*

---

### Section 3 — Where the Gap Lives

**Purpose:** Precise localization of the divergence between Section 1 and Section 2. This is the most important section of the report.

**Content:**
- **The primary gap** — A single paragraph. The most important place where public presence and business reality diverge. Not a list — a single central observation, written with maximum precision. If there is one thing the client takes away from the entire report, this is it.
- **Secondary gaps** — 2-3 specific observations about other points of divergence. Each with the exact place where it appears, what it signals relative to what exists in reality, and why it matters.
- **What amplifies the gap** — Factors in the market or in the client's behavior that make the gap more visible or more costly than it would be under other conditions. Not judgment — observation.

**The question it answers:** *Where exactly does the divergence live — not in general, but at a specific, addressable point?*

---

### Section 4 — What the Gap Costs

**Purpose:** Concrete consequences — not abstract, not potential, but what is already happening.

**Content:**
- **Cost in pricing conversations** — How the gap affects bargaining power and the type of pricing conversation the client has now versus what they would have with a presence aligned to reality.
- **Cost in client quality** — What type of client arrives now versus what type should be arriving. Not in demographic terms — in terms of fit, understanding, and ease of relationship.
- **Cost in market authority** — How the gap affects the way the client is perceived by peers, partners, potential employees, or investors. Not just by clients.
- **Cost in energy** — Repeated explanations. Conversations that should not be necessary. Time spent justifying what should be obvious. The most invisible cost and the most real one.

**The question it answers:** *What is this gap costing the business — in terms that can be felt, not just described?*

---

### Section 5 — What Needs to Happen Next

**Purpose:** Direction — without execution recommendations, without roadmap, without pitch.

**Content:**
- **What needs to be solved first** — A single priority. Not a list of actions — a clear direction about where any intervention must begin and why.
- **What should not be touched until the first thing is resolved** — As important as the priority. If the client acts in the wrong order, they waste resources and deepen the gap.
- **The question they need to ask themselves** — Not a recommendation. A precise question the client needs to take forward — alone or with help. The question that, if answered clearly, unlocks everything else.

**The question it answers:** *What is the one thing that must happen first — and what should absolutely not happen until it does?*

---

## The Final Paragraph

A short closing. 2-3 sentences. Not a summary. Not a CTA.

Tone: an observer who has said what they saw and leaves the decision where it belongs — with the client.

---

## What the Report Is Not

- Not a free consultation repackaged as a product
- Not a sample designed to sell something larger
- Not a mood board, brand audit checklist, or competitor analysis
- Not a strategy, a visual recommendation, or a roadmap
- Not a pitch for the next engagement

It is a diagnosis, complete in itself. If what the client needs after reading it is a full positioning engagement — that conversation exists. But it is a separate decision, made after clarity, not before.

---

*Delivered within 7 days of the discovery session. Written. Complete. No open questions left unanswered.*
