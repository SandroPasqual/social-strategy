# Discovery Session — Goodspell

> **Client:** Sandro Pasqual (Goodspell)
> **Duration:** 60 minutes
> **Date:** 2026-06-20
> **Session type:** Perception Audit

---

## Pre-Call Preparation

Questionnaire reviewed the day before. Marked areas:

- **Vague/evasive:** The description of "what's missing from presence" is accurate but avoids naming the emotional cost — not just practical, but what it feels like to have the business be invisible
- **Tension mentioned but not developed:** The gap between personal (Sandro) and business (Goodspell) — the strategy says they're separate, but publicly they function as one
- **Contradiction:** The system is described as "complete" but the public presence is "underbuilt" — how complete is a system if it can't be seen?
- **What wasn't said:** No mention of competitors, no mention of what "success" looks like for Goodspell's own presence, no mention of revenue targets

---

## Minute 0-5 — Calibration

**Opening question:**

> "Reading what you wrote, I identified a few places where I'd like to go deeper. Before we do that — is there anything you wanted to write in the form but didn't?"

**Response:**

A pause. Then:

> "I wrote that the system is complete. It's not. The methodology is complete. The public face of it isn't. I didn't write that because it sounds like I'm making excuses for myself the way clients do when they say 'we know we need this, we just haven't gotten to it.'"

**Observation:** The first contradiction appears immediately. The questionnaire says "the system is complete." The real answer is more honest — and more interesting.

**Marked for the report:** The client knows the gap. He just needed permission to name it.

---

## Minute 5-25 — Business Reality

**Q1:** *"You said you're difficult to replace because of the system. When a client does leave — what actually happened?"*

**A:** "No client has left. But one engagement ended — the founder sold the company. The system they built with Goodspell was part of the due diligence package. The acquirer asked: 'Who built this?' That's when I knew the system was working — it was visible enough to be an asset in a transaction. But the acquirer found it through the client, not through Goodspell. The system added value. Goodspell didn't get the credit."

**Observation:** This is the gap in miniature. The work is real. The recognition flows to the client, not to Goodspell. That's by design (confidentiality), but it has a cost — Goodspell's reputation grows slower than the quality of its work.

---

**Q2:** *"Describe the best client you've ever had. What made them the best?"*

**A:** "The one who didn't need to be convinced. They read three posts, recognized the problem, booked the audit, completed the questionnaire in 48 hours, and showed up to the session ready. They treated the engagement the way they treat their own business — seriously, without needing handholding."

**Observation:** The best client is the one who needs the least from Sandro before the work begins. This confirms the positioning: the right client recognizes the situation immediately.

---

**Q3:** *"Is there something in your business that you consider obvious and that others treat as remarkable?"*

**A:** "The methodology. I built GBS over 15 years. Every document, every section, every number has a reason. To me, it's just the way I work. To everyone who sees it, it's the most organized thing they've ever encountered in brand strategy. The gap between 'this is how I think' and 'this is extraordinary' is invisible to me."

**Observation:** This is both an asset and a blind spot. The rigor that makes Goodspell exceptional is also what makes Sandro underestimate its rarity.

---

**Q4:** *"What have you refused to do, even though the market would have paid you for it?"*

**A:** "Logos. Websites. Taglines. Brand books that look good on a shelf. Hourly billing. Custom proposals for every prospect. I've walked away from projects that would have paid 3x the audit fee because they wanted decoration, not diagnosis. The market rewards decoration. I refuse to sell it."

**Observation:** The refusal is a positioning choice — but it's also why the public presence is underbuilt. Decoration is easy to show. Diagnosis is invisible until someone has experienced it.

---

## Minute 25-45 — The Gap and Its Cost

**Q5:** *"You mentioned the website is in draft. What exactly happened in that moment when someone landed on it and asked 'is this active?' — what did you feel was missing?"*

**A:** "I felt like the work I'd done didn't exist. Someone standing on goodspell.online has no idea about the GBS system, the portal, the 30 documents, the clients who've used it. They see a draft. That's it. The gap between what I've built and what they see is so wide that the two don't feel connected. It's not that the website is incomplete — it's that the website communicates nothing about the scale of what exists."

**Observation:** This is the purest articulation of the gap for Goodspell itself. The internal reality is a complete operating system. The external signal is a draft.

---

**Q6:** *"The conversation you have repeatedly — 'so what exactly do you do?' — who usually initiates it? The client or you?"*

**A:** "I do. Every prospect who reaches out asks a version of this. Even the ones who've read my LinkedIn for months. They know how I think. They don't know what I offer. I have to explain it — which means I'm justifying the existence of the business in every first conversation."

**Observation:** The personal brand (The Observer) generates recognition. But it doesn't convert that recognition into understanding of the offering. The bridge between "how I think" and "what I do" doesn't exist publicly.

---

**Q7:** *"If tomorrow your public presence perfectly reflected what you've built — what would be the first concrete thing that would change?"*

**A:** "The first call with a prospect would be shorter by 20 minutes. Right now, I spend the first third of every call establishing that Goodspell is real, that the methodology exists, that there's a system here. If the presence did that work, the call would start from alignment — not from 'let me explain what I do.'"

**Observation:** Time is the most measurable cost. 20 minutes per call × number of prospects = significant operational drag.

---

**Q8:** *"Is there someone in your market whose public presence you respect? What do they do differently?"*

**A:** "Yes. A handful of strategists who've built proper company presences — not just personal brands. What they do differently: they have a website that explains what they offer in the same voice as their personal content. The bridge exists. The personal content generates recognition. The company site confirms it and converts it. I have the personal content. I don't have the bridge."

**Observation:** The diagnosis is clear. The bridge is missing. Goodspell has the destination (the system, the portal, the methodology) but not the path that leads there from Sandro's personal presence.

---

## Minute 45-55 — Decision Context

**Q9:** *"Why now and not 2 years ago?"*

**A:** "Two years ago, the system didn't exist. I couldn't show what I didn't have. Now the system exists. The only thing missing is the public presence that reflects it. The gap is no longer about building — it's about showing."

**Observation:** Clean answer. No hedging. The timing is driven by readiness, not by urgency.

---

**Q10:** *"What would it mean for you to complete this audit and do nothing with it?"*

**A:** "It would mean the system is complete but silent. Which is the same thing as not existing. If Goodspell diagnoses gaps for clients and doesn't close its own — that's not strategy, that's blindness."

**Observation:** Strong reaction. The answer shows seriousness. The client isn't buying peace of mind — he's buying action.

---

## Minute 55-60 — Closing

**Closing statement:**

> "I have everything I need. You will receive the document in 7 days."

No promises about what it will contain. No preview. No questions about whether there's anything else to add — 55 minutes was enough.

## Session Summary

### Contradictions identified
1. Questionnaire says "system is complete" — session reveals the public face isn't
2. Methodology is exceptional — but Sandro treats it as normal
3. Personal brand is strong — business brand is underbuilt
4. Confidentiality protects clients — but starves Goodspell's own reputation

### Tensions to explore in the report
- The cost of the missing bridge between Personal and Goodspell
- The operational drag of re-explaining the offering in every first call
- The gap between the rigor of the system and the thinness of the presence
- What it costs Goodspell to be invisible at its own price point

### Key material for the report
- The acquirer who found the system through the client, not through Goodspell
- 20 minutes per call spent on re-establishing credibility
- The contradiction between "complete system" and "underbuilt presence"
- The refusal to sell decoration — and its impact on visibility
