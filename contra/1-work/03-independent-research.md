# Independent Research — Goodspell

> **Internal document.** Raw material. Not presented to the client.
> **Researcher:** Sandro Pasqual
> **Date:** 2026-06-20
> **Engagement:** Perception Audit — Goodspell

---

## Research Scope

Analysis of Goodspell's public presence conducted independently after the discovery session. Covers:

- Public touchpoints (website, LinkedIn, Contra)
- Market positioning relative to competitors
- The gap visible from the outside (without access to internal context)

---

## 1. Public Touchpoints — What an External Observer Finds

### 1.1 goodspell.online (Website)

**Current state:** Draft. The homepage copy exists as a markdown file (`site/copy/homepage.md`) but the live site is not fully operational.

**What it signals:** A practice that is under construction. The copy itself is strong — it reads as finished — but the absence of a proper live site communicates that the business is not yet active or not yet serious about its own presence.

**The contradiction:** The homepage copy is some of the best in the category. It names the situation precisely, avoids fluff, maintains consistent voice. But a prospect who lands on a site that feels unfinished will assume the business is unfinished — regardless of how good the text is.

**Signal received by the market:** "Interesting thinking. Not sure if they're open for business."

---

### 1.2 LinkedIn — Goodspell Page

**Current state:** 20 posts published. Currently paused. Voice: The Architect.

**What it signals:** A company page that started active but stopped. The content is consistent with the positioning — posts target the same gap, same audience, same tone as the personal account. But the pause creates uncertainty: is this business active?

**Observation:** The posts are good. They would generate recognition. But a page that stops posting communicates either (a) the business ran out of steam, or (b) it's deprioritized. Neither signal works for a consultancy asking $6k–$12k per engagement.

**Signal received by the market:** "They had something to say. They stopped saying it."

---

### 1.3 LinkedIn — Personal (Sandro Pasqual)

**Current state:** 68+ posts. Active (Mon/Wed/Fri). Voice: The Observer. Growing engagement.

**What it signals:** A serious, consistent thinker. The quality is high. The frequency is disciplined. The voice is distinctive — anyone who reads one post would recognize another.

**The bridge problem:** The personal account does not reference Goodspell by name. By design: "zero selling on Personal." But this means a reader who is impressed by the thinking has no obvious path to the offering. They can infer, search, connect dots — but the inference is not guaranteed. Most readers will not connect dots they don't know exist.

**Signal received by the market:** "This person thinks clearly about brand. Is this a service or just a hobby?"

---

### 1.4 Contra Profile

**Current state:** Just created. Bio written, rate set ($100–150/hr), Perception Audit service ready to publish.

**What it signals:** A new entry. No reviews, no completed projects, no history on the platform.

**The opportunity:** Contra is fresh — it can be built from the ground up as the bridge between Personal and Goodspell. The bio already reflects the same voice. The service is the first step.

**Signal received by the market:** (Nothing yet — nothing is published.)

## 2. Market Positioning — Competitor Landscape

### 2.1 High-End Brand Strategists on Contra

**Andrea DiNardo** — Brand Strategist, $100–150/hr (estimated). Portfolio shows client work (copy, voice, messaging). Active presence. Good positioning but shares some client details.

**Observation:** Andrea shows the work. Goodspell doesn't. Andrea gets visibility. Goodspell gets confidentiality. Both are valid positions — but they lead to different levels of public presence.

### 2.2 Brand Designers (the category Goodspell is NOT in)

Most profiles on Contra in "brand" are designers — they show logos, packaging, websites, visual identity. They have clear portfolios because their work is visual.

Goodspell's work is invisible. It cannot be shown. This is a structural difference in the category, not a performance gap.

**The market's expectation:** Brand = visual. Goodspell violates this expectation. That's the point of the positioning — but it also means the portfolio cannot follow the conventions of the category.

### 2.3 The Gap in the Market

No one on Contra is doing what Goodspell does at the level Goodspell does it. The brand strategy category is dominated by:
- Designers who call themselves strategists (portfolio: logos)
- Coaches who call themselves strategists (portfolio: frameworks)
- Agency refugees who call themselves strategists (portfolio: decks)

No one has a documented, indexed, tokenized methodology. No one has a portal. No one operates at this level of structural rigor.

**The opportunity:** Goodspell is alone at the top of the category. The problem: no one knows it exists yet.

## 3. The Gap Visible from the Outside

Without access to internal context, an external observer would see:

| What exists internally | What is visible externally | The gap |
|----------------------|---------------------------|---------|
| Complete GBS methodology (30+ indexed documents) | Nothing — no mention of methodology exists publicly | **The system is invisible** |
| Custom client portal | Nothing | **The infrastructure is invisible** |
| Proprietary visual identity system | A draft website with no identity applied | **The identity exists but isn't shown** |
| 25 years of strategic experience | A LinkedIn personal account with 1 year of active posting | **The depth is invisible** |
| Client results (confidential) | Zero case studies, zero testimonials, zero logos | **The proof is invisible** |
| Clear positioning (The Architect) | Goodspell page paused, Personal page doesn't reference it | **The offering is ambiguous** |

### The Core Insight

Goodspell has solved the problem for clients but not for itself. The system that diagnoses and closes gaps for others has not been applied to its own public presence. This is not a failure — it's a sequence problem. The infrastructure needed to exist before the presence. Now the infrastructure exists. The presence is the next phase.

### The External Observer's Conclusion

> "This is someone who clearly knows what they're doing. I can tell from how they write. But I can't tell what they offer, whether they're active, or what I would actually get if I hired them. The thinking is visible. The business is not."
