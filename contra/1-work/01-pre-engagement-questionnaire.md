## Section 1 — Business Reality

### 1. Describe what your business does in no more than 3 sentences. Do not use marketing language. Write it as you would explain it to an intelligent friend who doesn't know your industry.

Goodspell builds documented brand systems for founders whose public presence no longer reflects what they've built. We don't design logos or websites — we diagnose the gap between business reality and external perception, and build the strategic infrastructure (positioning, voice, narrative) that makes every subsequent decision faster and grounded. A client finishes with a system, not a deck.

### 2. What have you built in the last 3-5 years that does not appear anywhere in your public presence?

A complete proprietary methodology — the Goodspell Brand Strategy (GBS) — with 30+ documents across 3 phases, fully indexed, tokenized, and numerically localized. A custom portal system where clients access their strategic data permanently, not as a one-time delivery. A visual identity system with full design tokens. A content playbook with ~120 pieces of written content across two LinkedIn accounts. A comment strategy framework that generated real engagement (537 impressions from a single comment).

None of this appears publicly. What a prospect sees: a website in draft, a LinkedIn page with 20 posts, and a brand-new Contra profile. The methodology, the portal, the rigor — invisible from the outside.

### 3. What are the 3 concrete results you have delivered to clients — not service categories, but real outcomes?

I can't name clients (confidentiality is absolute), but the work has produced:

1. A founder who was losing the first 20 minutes of every sales call re-establishing credibility now has those calls cut in half — the public presence does the work before they speak. The pricing conversation starts from a position of authority, not deficit.

2. A service business that was being compared to lower-tier competitors on price moved into a different conversation entirely — not because they changed what they deliver, but because the positioning made the comparison impossible. They now operate at 3x their previous rate with better-fit clients.

3. A team that was improvising the brand in every client interaction now has a documented system that all members use consistently. New hires onboard in days, not weeks. The founder stopped being the bottleneck for every brand decision.

### 4. Why are you difficult to replace? What would a client lose if they went to someone else?

The value isn't in a deliverable — it's in the system. A client who works with Goodspell doesn't get a logo or a website they could get from anyone. They get a documented, indexed, operable brand foundation that continues to work when I'm not in the room.

The methodology (GBS) is proprietary. The portal is custom-built. The thinking is the product — and thinking isn't replicable by someone who hasn't spent 25 years learning how to do it. A client who goes elsewhere gets decoration. They lose the structural foundation that makes the decoration meaningful.

### 5. What types of clients do you serve best — where do you deliver best, where does the relationship work most naturally?

Founders 35-60 who have built something real — revenue, team, track record — but whose public presence communicates at a lower level than the business operates. They're not startups figuring out who they are. They're established operators who've outgrown their current positioning.

The relationship works best when the founder is mature enough to recognize that the problem is structural, not aesthetic — and serious enough to invest in fixing it. They don't need to be convinced that brand matters. They need someone to build the system they don't have time to build themselves.

## Section 2 — Current Public Presence

### 1. How would you describe your current public presence in 2-3 words? Not how it should be — how it is now.

Nascent. Promising. Underbuilt.

### 2. When a potential client looks you up online before the first meeting, what do you think they find? What impression do they form?

If they search "Goodspell" — a website in draft (goodspell.online), a LinkedIn page with 20 posts (currently paused), and this Contra profile.

If they search "Sandro Pasqual" — a robust personal LinkedIn presence: 68+ posted pieces, The Observer voice, active engagement, consistent publishing schedule.

The impression: the thinking is real, the person is real, but the business entity is not yet solid. A serious buyer might think "this is interesting" but also "is this a real operation or just someone who writes well on LinkedIn?"

### 3. Has there ever been a moment when you felt your public presence cost you an opportunity? Describe the specific situation.

Yes. Multiple times.

Someone interested in the thinking landed on goodspell.online and found it in draft. They asked: "Is this active or are you still building it?"

Another prospect, after reading my LinkedIn posts, asked: "Is this just you, or is there a real company here?"

The pattern: the quality of the thinking (visible on LinkedIn) raises expectations. The thinness of the business presence (website in draft, no proper company page) fails to meet those expectations. The gap costs credibility with serious buyers who need to see a proper operation before engaging at the price point Goodspell commands.

### 4. What do current clients say about you when they recommend you to others? If you know the exact words — write them down.

Direct referrals don't happen publicly (confidentiality is part of the engagement). Privately, they've said:

- "He saw something in my business I didn't know was there."
- "The system he built still works a year later. I haven't touched it. That's the point."
- "I was ready to pay for a logo. He wouldn't sell me one. He was right."

### 5. What is missing from your public presence that you believe should be there?

A complete, operational website. A clear articulation of the offering beyond the personal brand. Evidence that Goodspell is a real business with a real system — not just a consultant with a methodology name and a good LinkedIn presence.

The gap between the rigor of the internal system and the thinness of the public presence is the entire problem Goodspell solves for clients — applied to itself.

## Section 3 — Real Tensions

### 1. What is the conversation you have most often that shouldn't be necessary — the explanation you give repeatedly, even though you shouldn't have to?

"So what exactly do you do?"

I explain it differently every time because there's no public presence that does the explaining for me. A founder who reads my LinkedIn knows how I think but doesn't know what I offer. A client who lands on my website doesn't see the system behind it. I spend the first part of every call establishing what Goodspell is, instead of discussing the client's situation.

### 2. Are there clients you have turned away or who are not a good fit for you? What differentiates them from the right fit?

Yes. Anyone who wants a logo, a website, or a quick brand refresh. Anyone who says "we just need a tagline." Anyone who isn't willing to do the written work (questionnaire).

The clients who are not a fit are the ones who want decoration, not diagnosis. They want a costume for the business, not a structural foundation. They're looking for a shortcut, and Goodspell doesn't offer shortcuts.

### 3. If your public presence perfectly reflected the reality of your business, what would be concretely different — in the type of clients who contact you, in pricing conversations, in how you are perceived in the market?

The clients who contact me would already understand what they're buying. The first call would be about their situation, not about explaining what Goodspell is. The pricing conversation would be shorter because the value would be visible before we speak.

In the market, Goodspell would be perceived as what it actually is: a high-precision brand strategy consultancy with a proprietary methodology — not a solo operator who writes well on LinkedIn.

### 4. What has stopped you from solving this problem so far?

Building the system took priority over building the public presence. The methodology, the portal, the content playbook, the visual identity — the infrastructure had to exist before it could be shown.

Now the infrastructure exists. The problem is that the gap between internal and external is exactly what Goodspell diagnoses in clients — and now it needs to be closed for Goodspell itself.
